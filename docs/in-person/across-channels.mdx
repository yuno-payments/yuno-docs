A customer who buys in your store and a customer who buys on your website are usually two records, in two systems, with no way to tell they are the same person. In-Person Payments on Yuno makes them one: the same customer, the same card on file and the same subscription, whichever channel the sale starts in.

This is what the **Unify** package adds on top of whichever [integration type](/docs/in-person/ways-to-integrate) you run.

<Note>
  **Certification.** Yuno certifies the chain for each acquirer and each market, card-present PCI requirements included. Tell your Yuno contact which markets and acquirers you need.
</Note>

## One customer, in store and online

Every card carries a **payment account reference** (PAR): an identifier of the card account that is not the card number and is safe to store. Yuno takes it from the authorisation response, in store and online alike, and links both to the same `customer_payer` — the same [customer](/docs/basic-concepts/customers) object you already see in online payments. The link is made **inside your own Yuno account only**; a customer is never matched across merchants.

For you this means:

- You know who pays in store: repeat customers, how often they come, which stores they visit.
- In-store and online purchases of the same customer sit side by side, for loyalty, analytics and customer service.
- A refund or a dispute can be traced to the customer, whatever the channel of the original purchase.
- A returning customer can tap the card they enrolled with and the sale is charged to the account already on file, with nothing to key in.

The link is made on card data alone. To attach your own customer ID to an in-store sale, send it in the sale's metadata from your checkout system.

<Note>
  **PAR is not a card number and cannot be charged.** It identifies the account behind a card so two sales can be recognised as the same customer. Charging a saved card is a separate thing, and it needs the customer's agreement — see below.
</Note>

## The card on file

When the customer agrees, the card is kept as a **vaulted token** in Yuno's vault, the same vault your online payments already use. The token is provisioned from the sale at the counter itself, so the card does not have to be presented again to be saved. Your systems hold the token; the card number never reaches them.

| | What it is | Can it charge? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Payment account reference** | An identifier of the card account, read from the card in both channels | No. It links the customer, nothing else |
| **Vaulted token** | The card itself, stored in Yuno's vault once the customer agrees | Yes, in either channel |

Because the vault is central to your Yuno account rather than tied to one processor, a token created at the counter is the same token your website uses, and it keeps working if you add or change acquirers. The detail of how tokens are issued and stored is on [Tokens](/docs/basic-concepts/tokens).

Later charges are made against that vaulted token. A [network token](/docs/security-and-compliance/network-tokens) is a different thing, used in the authorisation itself, and is not what a subscription or a follow-on charge is anchored to.

What this gives you in practice:

- A card saved at the counter can be charged online, and the reverse.
- Follow-on charges and tips on a sale that already happened.
- Subscriptions that start in store and bill online, below.

The PIN, the full card number, the expiry date and the card's cryptogram never reach your checkout system or your servers. Card data leaves the terminal encrypted, and it is kept only as a token, only when the customer agrees. See [Coverage and FAQ](/docs/in-person/coverage-and-faq) for how this sits with PCI.

## Subscriptions started in store

A customer can start a subscription at the counter: the POS terminal takes the first payment, and the customer's agreement to keep the card travels with that sale so the card can be vaulted from it.

**Your own system creates the subscription**, once that payment has cleared, against the vaulted token the sale produced. Yuno does not create it for you — which means a sale that declines simply leaves no subscription behind, and you decide what the counter staff see before anything recurring exists. Yuno bills every cycle after that.

The in-store payment and the online charges share the same `customer_payer` and appear together in the dashboard.

One customer can hold several subscriptions on the same card — a membership bought for each of four family members at the counter is four subscriptions against the same customer and the same vaulted token, each billed and cancelled on its own.

This is the two-step route the online docs already describe: you hold a vaulted token for the customer, then call [Create Subscription](/reference/subscriptions/create-subscription) with it. What differs in store is only where the token comes from — the sale at the counter rather than an online enrolment. It is *not* the one-call route on [Start a subscription with the first payment](/docs/payment-features/subscriptions/start-with-payment), where Yuno creates the subscription on the back of the charge itself.

Everything after that is an ordinary Yuno subscription: the same [plans](/docs/payment-features/subscriptions/plans), the same [retries](/docs/payment-features/subscriptions/retries) and the same endpoints to pause, resume or cancel it. See [Subscriptions](/docs/payment-features/subscriptions/index).

<Note>
  **Who initiates matters.** The sale at the counter is customer-initiated: the cardholder is standing there and authenticates on the terminal. The renewals that follow are merchant-initiated, sent on the stored credential without the customer present. The scheme rules for that, and how Yuno complies with them, are on [Stored Credentials](/docs/payment-features/stored-credentials).
</Note>

<Warning>
  **In the EU, the first charge has to carry strong customer authentication.** Under PSD2 the customer-initiated sale that establishes the credential must be authenticated — chip and PIN qualifies, and so does a wallet such as Apple Pay. A contactless tap that asks for no cardholder verification does not, and cannot be used to start a subscription.
</Warning>
