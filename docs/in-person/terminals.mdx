Yuno's payment app runs on the terminals you already own. This page covers which terminals, how the app is enrolled and updated, what you can brand, and what you see about the fleet.

## Supported terminals

| Vendor | Model | Form factor | Status | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ingenico | AXIUM 5000 series | Countertop PIN pad, Android | Available | Receipt data goes to the POS; no built-in printer on RX units |
| Ingenico | AXIUM 7000 series | Countertop PIN pad, Android | Available | Same app, same configuration |
| Verifone | Android terminals | Countertop and portable | Coming soon | Same app with a Verifone adapter |

The app talks to each vendor's certified kernel through an adapter, so a new vendor is a new adapter, not a new app. If your fleet has a model that is not listed, tell your Yuno contact; the terminal has to be an Android device with a PCI PTS PIN pad and a current EMV Level 1 and 2 approval.

## How the app reaches a terminal

<Steps>
  <Step title="Registration">
    Each terminal is registered to a store and a lane in Yuno. Only registered terminals can talk to Yuno Cloud, over a secure channel with an identity per device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Installation">
    Yuno's app is signed and distributed to the fleet through the vendor's or your own device management, as any terminal app is today. You keep ownership and maintenance of the hardware.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keys">
    Each acquirer's encryption keys are injected by the acquirer or the vendor, never by Yuno. A terminal holds one key per acquirer it can route to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configuration">
    Store, enabled payment methods, instalment plans, brand and routing rules are pushed from Yuno Cloud. The terminal keeps the last configuration it received, so it works through short outages.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go live">
    The terminal reports online, appears in the fleet view, and takes its first sale. From then on it sends health, app version and last sale to the dashboard.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you can brand, and what never changes

The app has two layers. The certified core (reading the card, taking the PIN, building the message to the acquirer) is frozen once certified, because any change to it restarts the acquirer and card-brand certification. Everything a customer or cashier sees sits outside it and is configuration.

| Configurable per merchant, no recertification | Frozen after certification |
| --- | --- |
| Logo, colours and wordmark on every screen, including the idle screen between sales | Reading the chip or contactless card |
| Screen texts and languages (Spanish first) | PIN capture on the secure keypad |
| Receipt text and layout | The message to each acquirer |
| Enabled payment methods and instalment plans | Kernel configuration: verification methods, floor limits, terminal type |
| Routing rules per store | Adding an acquirer (a new certification, once per acquirer) |
| Telemetry and health reporting | |

White label is the default: no Yuno branding on the terminal unless you ask for it.

## Network requirements

- The terminal needs outbound HTTPS (TCP 443) to `*.y.uno` for configuration, rules and sale reporting, and to the acquirer's host for authorisation. No inbound connections and nothing to open on your firewall for Yuno.
- The Local type uses your store network between the POS and the terminal; Yuno is not on that path.
- Wi-Fi, Ethernet or cellular, as the terminal supports; a terminal with two paths fails over between them on its own.

## Updates

Releases that touch only the configurable layer ship like any app update: staged to a group of terminals first, then to the fleet, with the version visible per terminal in the dashboard. Releases that touch the certified core are rare, planned with the acquirers, and certified before they ship. Routing rule changes are not app releases at all; they are configuration pushed from Yuno Cloud and picked up by each terminal when it next connects.
