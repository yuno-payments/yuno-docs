## Coverage

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Regions** | Latin America and Europe. |
| **Acquirers** | The acquirers you already work with. Yuno integrates and certifies the Yuno Payment App with each new acquirer on demand, following the acquirer's specification. |
| **Card brands** | Visa and Mastercard; other card schemes on demand. |
| **POS terminals** | Android POS terminals that accept external payment applications, from vendors such as Verifone and Ingenico. New vendors are added as adapters to the same app. |
| **Payment methods** | Chip and PIN, contactless, instalments. Local methods such as QR payments and wallets are integrated on demand, following each provider's API specification. |
| **Integration types** | Local, cloud, standalone and reconciliation first. |
| **Beyond the sale** | Subscriptions started in store, one customer view across in-store and online, reconciliation per store and acquirer. |

## Certification and compliance

|  | What it is | Where Yuno stands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **EMV Level 1 and 2** | The terminal hardware and kernel, approved by EMVCo | The vendor's, unchanged: the Yuno Payment App uses the kernel through the vendor's SDK and never modifies it |
| **Level 3 / card-brand certification** | The full chain terminal, app and acquirer, tested end to end per acquirer and brand (Mastercard M-TIP, Visa) | The Yuno Payment App is certified with each acquirer, which signs the certification. Adding an acquirer is a new certification; adding a merchant is not |
| **Service provider registration** | Each acquirer registers the providers in its chain with Visa and Mastercard | Done by each acquirer for Yuno |
| **PCI PTS** | The PIN pad hardware | The vendor's, unchanged |
| **PCI DSS** | How Yuno handles card data | In-person payments run inside Yuno's PCI DSS programme |
| **PCI PIN** | Transmitting, decrypting or translating PINs | The PIN goes encrypted from the PIN pad to the acquirer and never passes through Yuno's systems, so Yuno doesn't decrypt or translate PINs |

What restarts certification and what doesn't is on [POS terminals and fleet](/docs/in-person/terminals).

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do we need to buy new POS terminals?">
    Not if your terminals are Android devices whose vendor allows external payment applications. If you are mid-migration to a new fleet, that is the easiest moment to add the app. Yuno doesn't sell or lease terminals.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is the difference between the POS terminal and the checkout system?">
    The POS terminal is the card device on the counter, where the Yuno Payment App runs and the customer taps or inserts the card. The checkout system is your cash-register software, where the cashier rings up the basket and which tells the terminal how much to charge. In the Standalone type there is no checkout system involved: the cashier keys the amount on the terminal.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do we need to change acquirers?">
    No. You keep your contracts, your merchant IDs and your rates. Yuno routes between the acquirers you work with; if one is not integrated yet, Yuno integrates and certifies it on demand.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="If the Yuno Payment App chooses the acquirer, what does Yuno Cloud do?">
    The app makes the decision on the terminal, but with the rules you publish in Yuno Cloud and the acquirer health that Yuno Cloud computes from every terminal of every store. Yuno Cloud also records every payment, sends your webhooks, runs reconciliation, keeps customers and subscriptions, and manages the fleet. It is never in the authorisation, so the store keeps selling if it can't reach Yuno.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does Yuno touch the card data or the PIN?">
    The card is read and the PIN is entered on the terminal's certified hardware, encrypted to the acquirer. Yuno never sees the PIN. Card data only leaves the terminal encrypted, and it is kept only as a token when the customer agrees to a subscription.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if the store loses its connection to Yuno?">
    The POS terminal keeps the latest routing rules and continues to authorise directly with the acquirer. Sales are reported to Yuno when the connection returns, with their original time and ID.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a declined payment be retried with another acquirer?">
    Not in store. The PIN is encrypted for the acquirer chosen before the customer typed it, and an authorisation only ever goes to one acquirer, so the result of that sale is final. Routing across sales is where the benefit is: cost, instalment programmes and staying up when an acquirer is down.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do voids and refunds follow the routing rules?">
    No. They always go to the acquirer that approved the original sale.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can we see the same customer in store and online?">
    Yes. Yuno links in-store and online payments made with the same card to the same customer, through the card's payment account reference, without the card number reaching your systems. See [Payments and events](/docs/in-person/payments-and-events).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a customer pay part with one card and part with another?">
    Yes. Each card is its own payment, sent one after the other with the same order reference. Each is routed and authorised on its own; if the second declines, the first stands.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="We are a platform and our merchants own the terminals. How do we integrate?">
    With the Cloud type: your server creates the sale and a registered POS terminal in the store collects it. Reconciliation first can connect your merchants' acquirers before their terminals switch.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
