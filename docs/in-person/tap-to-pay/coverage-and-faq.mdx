## Coverage

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Android** | Phones and tablets with NFC, on a supported Android version, with Google Play services. Rooted or modified devices are blocked by the app's security checks |
| **iPhone** | iPhone XS or later, on a supported iOS version, in the markets where Apple offers Tap to Pay on iPhone: see [Apple's list of supported regions](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/regions/) |
| **Markets** | Mexico, Brazil and Ecuador. More markets on demand, under the same agreement |
| **Card brands** | Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover and local brands. Elo is coming soon |
| **Wallets** | Apple Pay, Google Pay and other contactless wallets on the customer's phone or watch |
| **Acquirers** | The acquirers you work with. Yuno integrates and certifies new acquirers on demand |
| **Instalments** | Where the acquirer offers them, such as parcelado in Brazil |

<Warning>
  **iPhone availability depends on Apple.** Tap to Pay on iPhone only works in the countries where Apple offers it: see [Apple's list of supported regions](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/regions/). In a market Apple does not cover yet, sellers there take payments on Android phones. Check with your Yuno contact before you promise iPhone in a new market.
</Warning>

## Certification and compliance

With the Yuno Tap to Pay app (on its own or opened from your app), you don't certify anything: Yuno holds and maintains the certifications of the app that takes the payment, and each acquirer certifies its connection once, with Yuno. With the SDK in your own app, your app is also certified before going live; see [Integration types](/docs/in-person/tap-to-pay/ways-to-integrate).

|  | What it is | Where it stands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PCI MPoC** | The PCI standard for accepting cards and PINs on a standard phone | The Yuno Tap to Pay app on Android runs on an MPoC-certified solution, and app updates and new features keep it. With the SDK, your Android app gets its own MPoC certification before going live |
| **Tap to Pay on iPhone** | Apple's own secure acceptance on iPhone | Apple provides the secure read and PIN entry; no MPoC applies on iPhone |
| **EMV contactless kernels** | The certified software that talks to the card, per brand | Part of the certified solution; Yuno never modifies it |
| **Level 3 / card-brand certification** | The full chain app to acquirer, tested end to end per acquirer and brand | Done once per acquirer, with Yuno. Adding an acquirer is a new certification; adding a merchant or a seller is not |
| **PCI DSS** | How card data is handled | Tap to Pay runs inside Yuno's PCI DSS Level 1 programme |
| **PCI PIN** | Transmitting, decrypting or translating PINs | The PIN is encrypted for the acquirer on the phone and travels to it; Yuno does not decrypt or translate it |

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do sellers need a card reader or any extra hardware?" icon="mobile-screen">
    No. A supported Android phone or iPhone is enough. The customer taps directly on it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can the app carry our brand and not Yuno's?" icon="palette">
    Yes, inside the app. The Yuno Tap to Pay app in the App Store and Google Play is one shared app, so its store listing, name and icon are neutral, not yours. When a seller signs in, the app loads your configuration: logo, colours, screen texts and receipts, set from the dashboard. Changing them does not restart any certification. If you need the app listed in the stores under your own name and icon, talk to your Yuno contact about timing and what it involves.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do we need to change acquirers?" icon="building-columns">
    No. You keep your contracts and merchant IDs. Yuno routes between the acquirers you work with; if one is not integrated yet, Yuno integrates and certifies it on demand.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a customer pay with a card that needs a PIN?" icon="lock">
    Yes. When the card, the amount or the market requires it, a secure PIN keypad appears on the screen. The PIN is encrypted as it is typed and never reaches your app or Yuno.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a declined tap be retried with another acquirer?" icon="route">
    Not for the same sale. The card data and the PIN are protected for the acquirer chosen before the tap, and an authorisation only goes to one acquirer. Routing across sales is where the benefit is: cost, instalments and staying up when an acquirer is down.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does Tap to Pay work offline?" icon="wifi">
    No. Each sale needs a data connection on the phone. Wi-Fi or mobile data both work.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we use Tap to Pay and POS terminals together?" icon="cash-register">
    Yes. Both produce `IN_PERSON` payments in the same account, with the same routing rules, webhooks and reconciliation. Filter on `in_person.device_type` to tell them apart.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="We want the tap inside our own app, not in a second app. Is that possible?" icon="code">
    Yes, with the Yuno Tap to Pay SDK. The seller never leaves your app, but your app is certified before going live (PCI MPoC on Android, Apple's approval on iPhone), and you ship SDK updates on Yuno's schedule. If you'd rather not certify your app, opening the Tap to Pay app from your app keeps your seller in your app until the tap and returns the result to it, with a short switch to the Yuno Tap to Pay app in between.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can we launch in a market that is not listed?" icon="globe">
    Yes, on Android. Tell your Yuno contact the market and the acquirer; new markets are added under the same agreement. On iPhone it depends on Apple: Tap to Pay on iPhone only works in the countries where Apple offers it ([Apple's list of supported regions](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/regions/)), so in a market Apple does not cover yet, sellers use Android phones until Apple adds it.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
